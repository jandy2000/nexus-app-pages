Privacy Policy

Lunadoku for iPhone. Last updated October 23, 2026.

The short version: Lunadoku does not ask for or store personal information about you. There are no accounts. The app sends anonymous usage statistics so we can improve the game, and you can turn them off. Players without the Core Pack see occasional ads from Google, which are non-personalised; Lunadoku never asks to track you. Your puzzles and your settings stay on your iPhone; your Nightly cards also sync through your own iCloud, which we never see.

What stays on your phone

  • Your games and cards. Your games in progress, the Nightly cards you have earned, your theme and your settings are stored on your iPhone in the app's own storage. They are not sent to us. Deleting the app deletes them; an iPhone backup includes them.
  • Your Nightly cards in iCloud. If you are signed in to iCloud, the app also keeps a copy of your Nightly cards in your own iCloud account (Apple's iCloud key-value storage), so your collection follows you to a new iPhone or a reinstall. Each card holds only the game: the night, its moon and theme, the difficulty, the finish, your time, corrections and hints, the order you placed your tiles, and when you earned it. The copy lives in your iCloud account, under Apple's privacy policy; we have no access to it and no server of ours is involved. Without iCloud, the cards stay on your iPhone only.
  • No camera, no microphone, no contacts, no photos and no precise location. The app never asks for them.

Purchases

The Core Pack and any theme are sold by Apple through the App Store. We never see your name, your Apple Account or your payment details. The app checks what you own with Apple directly on your iPhone; there is no server of ours in between.

Anonymous usage statistics

Lunadoku sends anonymous usage statistics (for example, that a Nightly was solved, or that the Core Pack was bought) to TelemetryDeck so we can improve the game. They carry a random identifier created on your device, never your name, contact details, advertising identifier or location, and they are not used for tracking or advertising. Each statistic also carries the app version, the iOS version, the device type (iPhone), your region setting and the day (not the time).

You can turn this off in the app (Menu > Settings > Privacy > Share Anonymous Statistics) or in the iPhone Settings app (Settings > Lunadoku > Share Anonymous Statistics). When you turn it off, the app sends one last note that statistics were turned off, deletes the random identifier, and sends nothing more.

Ads

Players without the Core Pack see an occasional full-screen ad, never while solving: only after a finished puzzle, at most one for every three puzzles, and none in your first days with the app. The Core Pack removes ads for good.

The ads come from Google AdMob. To show, limit and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, Google's software in the app may collect device identifiers, an approximate location worked out from your IP address, which ads you see and how you interact with them, and diagnostic data such as crashes and performance. Lunadoku asks Google for non-personalised ads and never shows Apple's request to track you, so your iPhone's advertising identifier is not available to the app or to Google.

In the EU, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks for your choice before any ad is requested, and you can change it at any time in Menu > Settings > Privacy > Privacy choices. How Google uses information from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

If you have chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass us anonymous crash reports and usage statistics; that is Apple's feature and you can turn it off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Children

Lunadoku is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. The anonymous statistics cannot be connected to you, so there is nothing for us to look up, correct or delete; turning them off deletes the random identifier on your iPhone. For requests about data held by Google, see Google's privacy policy linked above.
